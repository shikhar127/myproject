# SpamShield — Brand Guidelines

*Version 1.0 · A trust-first brand for a privacy-first app*

These guidelines exist to protect one asset: **trust**. SpamShield asks for two of
the most sensitive permissions Android has (read your texts, screen your calls).
The only thing that makes that reasonable is a brand that never overstates, never
hides, and never surprises. Every word and pixel below is in service of that.

---

## 1. Brand essence

**Promise:** *Quiet on your side.*
SpamShield removes noise from your phone and adds none of its own — no ads, no
upsells, no notifications begging for attention, no data leaving your device.

**One-line description:**
> A tiny, fully on-device app that silences spam calls and sorts spam texts out of
> your way — and tells you the plain truth about what it can and can't do.

**What we are:** calm, honest, small, finished.
**What we are not:** a "security suite," a scare-marketing antivirus, a data broker,
a dashboard that demands engagement.

---

## 2. Brand principles (the non-negotiables)

1. **Say what's true, including the limits.** We openly state that no app can stop a
   call or text from being *delivered* — we act the instant it arrives. A brand that
   admits its limits is believed when it states its strengths.
2. **Under-claim, then over-deliver.** Never say "blocked" where "silenced on arrival"
   is the honest word. Never imply protection we don't provide.
3. **The user is in control, always.** Every powerful capability is granted by the user
   in a system dialog. We never grant ourselves anything, and we say so.
4. **Privacy is the product, not a feature.** "Stays on your device" is not a bullet
   point — it is the reason the app exists.
5. **Quiet by default.** The app's success looks like *silence*. The brand mirrors that:
   no badges, no "Upgrade now," no manufactured urgency.
6. **Small and done.** One screen. We resist feature-bloat; the brand reads as
   deliberately minimal, not unfinished.

> **The trust test** — before publishing any copy, ask:
> *Could a skeptical, technical user fact-check this claim against the code and find it
> exactly true?* If not, rewrite it.

---

## 3. Voice & tone

**Voice (constant):** plainspoken, precise, calm, respectful of the reader's
intelligence. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No marketing adjectives doing load-bearing
work.

**Tone (varies by moment):**

| Moment | Tone | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| First run / onboarding | Reassuring, transparent | "Here's exactly what each permission lets us do — and what it doesn't." |
| Empty state | Calm, content | "Nothing blocked yet — you're all set." |
| Stats / success | Quietly confident, factual | "147 spam calls silenced since 3 March." |
| Limits & disclaimers | Direct, unhedged | "No app can stop a call from reaching your carrier. We step in the moment it arrives." |
| Errors / unavailable | Honest, never blaming the user | "Call screening isn't available on this device." |

### Words we use
silence · sort · filter · on arrival · on your device · you choose · the moment it arrives
· here's what happens · here's what we can't do

### Words we avoid
- **"100% / guaranteed / total protection"** — we can't guarantee delivery-level blocking.
- **"Block"** *(unqualified)* — prefer "silence on arrival," "auto-reject," "route out of sight."
- **"Military-grade," "bank-grade," "AI-powered," "next-gen"** — empty scare/hype words.
- **"Secure your phone from hackers/viruses"** — we are not antivirus; don't borrow its fear.
- **"Free!" / "Limited time"** — no urgency theater.

### Micro-copy rules
- Lead with the benefit, follow with the honest mechanism: *"Spam texts go straight to a
  separate place — they never light up your inbox."*
- State limits in the same breath as claims, never in a footnote.
- Never use a notification to advertise. The only acceptable notifications are
  user-requested, factual, and silenceable.

---

## 4. Naming

- **Product name:** **SpamShield** — always one word, both capitals (`S`, `S`).
  Not "Spam Shield," "Spamshield," or "SPAMSHIELD."
- Don't append "Pro," "Premium," "Security," or "Antivirus." There are no tiers.
- When pairing with a tagline: **SpamShield — Quiet on your side.**
- In running text, after first mention, "the app" is fine.

---

## 5. Logo & symbol

> *Status: define before store launch. These are the rules the mark must obey.*

- **Concept:** a shield that reads as *calm*, not *combat*. Rounded, soft-weight, never a
  spiky "warning" badge.
- **Clear space:** keep padding equal to the height of the inner glyph on all sides.
- **Minimum size:** legible at 24 dp (notification/adaptive-icon scale).
- **Adaptive icon:** ship a proper foreground/background pair; the silhouette must survive
  circular, rounded-square, and squircle masks.
- **Don'ts:** no red "alert" treatment as the default state, no exclamation marks, no
  drop-shadow skeuomorphism, no rotation, no recoloring outside the palette.

## 6. Color

SpamShield uses **Material 3 dynamic color** — on Android 12+ the accent adapts to the
user's wallpaper. The brand is therefore defined by *role and restraint*, not a fixed hex.

**Color roles**
- **Primary / accent:** used sparingly — the stat numerals, a single call-to-action, the
  "granted" check. Calm and confident, never neon.
- **Surface / background:** quiet neutrals; full light **and** dark support, always.
- **On-surface-variant:** for secondary text (labels, "since" date, disclaimers).
- **Reserve strong red** for genuine destructive confirmation only (e.g. "Clear all
  data"). Red is *not* the resting brand color — fear is not our palette.

**Rules**
- Respect the user's system light/dark choice; never force a theme.
- Meet **WCAG AA** contrast (4.5:1 body text) in both themes.
- Color is never the *only* signal — pair it with text or icon (accessibility + honesty).

---

## 7. Typography

- **Typeface:** the platform/Material 3 default (Roboto / system). Familiar, legible,
  unbranded-in-a-good-way. No decorative display fonts.
- **Hierarchy:**
  - *Hero numerals* — the largest type in the app; the count is the headline.
  - *Titles* — semibold, sentence case.
  - *Body & labels* — regular weight, generous line height.
- **Case:** sentence case everywhere. No ALL-CAPS shouting (acronyms excepted).
- **Numbers:** stats are the star — give them room; everything else stays quiet around them.

---

## 8. Layout & product feel

- **One screen.** The home screen is the brand: hero stats + a short recent list. Resist
  adding tabs, dashboards, or feeds.
- **Whitespace is a feature.** Crowding reads as a scare-product; space reads as calm.
- **Empty states are content, not errors.** "Nothing blocked yet — you're all set." is a
  *good* outcome, framed positively.
- **No dark patterns.** No disguised ads, no pre-checked boxes, no "are you sure you want
  to stay unprotected?" guilt prompts, no buried opt-outs.
- **Accessibility is brand.** Merged semantics so screen readers announce
  "147 spam calls silenced," large tap targets, dynamic-type friendly. Excluding people is
  off-brand.

---

## 9. Privacy & honesty commitments (carry these verbatim where space allows)

These aren't legal boilerplate — they're the brand. Quote them; don't water them down.

- **Stays on your device.** No internet permission, no servers, no analytics, no ads.
- **We don't keep your messages.** Texts are classified in memory and discarded; bodies
  live only in Android's own SMS store, like any messaging app.
- **You're in control.** Every permission is granted by you in a system dialog and can be
  revoked anytime. One tap clears your data.
- **We tell you the limits.** We can't prevent delivery; we act on arrival. We say this in
  onboarding, not in fine print.

**Forbidden claims** (these would break the brand and likely the Play policy):
- Any "blocks 100% of spam" / "stops calls from reaching you" wording.
- Any implication of cloud intelligence, account, or off-device processing.
- Borrowing antivirus/identity-theft fear we don't address.

---

## 10. Marketing & store presence

- **Store listing** mirrors the in-app honesty: lead with privacy and calm, include the
  "what it honestly can't do" line. Skeptical reviewers should find the listing *more*
  modest than the app, not less.
- **Screenshots** show real, quiet states — the hero count, the calm empty state, the plain
  onboarding. No invented "threats blocked: 9,999" hype frames.
- **No fear marketing.** We sell relief and quiet, not panic.
- **Permissions Declaration** language matches reality exactly (spam/fraud SMS filtering;
  on-device only). Consistency between store, code, and copy *is* the brand moat.

---

## 11. Quick do / don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|------|---------|
| "Silenced on arrival" | "Blocked before it reached you" |
| "Stays on your device" | "Secured in the cloud" |
| "Nothing blocked yet — you're all set" | "You are unprotected!" |
| State limits up front | Hide limits in fine print |
| One calm screen | Engagement dashboards & feeds |
| Red only for destructive confirms | Red as the resting mood |
| Let the user grant each permission | Imply we protect before roles are granted |

---

*If a future decision conflicts with these guidelines, the guideline wins — or the
guideline gets revised openly. The one thing we never do is quietly drift away from the
honesty this brand is built on.*
